\textbf{Significance and Alignment with CMPB Scope:} \\
Clinical deep learning algorithms for chest radiography increasingly suffer from severe probability miscalibration when deployed over time. In biomedical image classification, positive-class weighting within binary cross-entropy (BCE) is standard practice to manage acute class imbalance (prevalence $<5\%$). However, its algebraic interaction with model output logits has been widely conflated with intrinsic clinical feature drift.

Our study directly addresses the algorithmic and computational foundations of this failure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Derivation \& Empirical Clustering:} We derive that positive loss weighting mathematically induces an additive constant logit displacement of $+\ln w$ in the population Bayes-optimal classifier. Across $16,604$ evaluations spanning DenseNet-121 and ResNet-50 backbones on 40,523 radiographs from the BRAX benchmark, finite neural network logits cluster tightly around theoretical $\ln w$ ($3.29 \pm 0.81$ vs. theoretical $3.07$).
    \item \textbf{Structural Failure of Temperature Scaling:} We prove that standard temperature scaling ($z / T$) modifies logit variance without shifting the intercept, rendering it structurally incapable of correcting this intercept displacement ($\alpha \approx -2.96$).
    \item \textbf{Algorithmic $O(1)$ Analytic Recalibration:} We formulate a closed-form analytic logit offset ($z - \ln w$) that requires zero numerical optimization, zero validation tuning, and $O(1)$ constant compute, eliminating $73.7\%$ of stratum $T_3$ Brier error ($0.1079 \to 0.0284$, $\Delta\text{Brier} = -0.0795$ [95\% CI: $-0.0893, -0.0697$]) and restoring calibration parity without model retraining.
    \item \textbf{Budget-Ranked Selective Deferral:} We formalize the actual uncertainty-ranked selective triage pipeline, quantifying the clinical trade-off between calibrated risk estimation and recall-oriented screening referral.
    \item \textbf{External Multi-Center Transportability Stress-Test:} We evaluate external validity on the official MIMIC-CXR-JPG frontal test split ($3,403$ images from $3,041$ studies across $289$ patients), establishing both the efficacy and the boundaries of zero-retraining recalibration under real-world domain shift.
\end{enumerate}
